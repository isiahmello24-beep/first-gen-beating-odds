# Memo: Budgets explain 10.0% of beating the odds on first gen completion. Call the peers.

**To:** Director of Student Success. **From:** Isiah Luna. **Date:** October 2026

## How much is money?

Among 1,142 four year colleges, the top tenth graduated first gen students at least 10.4 points above prediction from their student mix. Adding spending level and mix explains **10.0%** of what that model left unexplained. Doubling core spending per student is associated with +6.5 points, one SD more academic support share with +1.1, one SD more instruction share with +1.6 (budget held fixed). All three are small against the 10.4 point gap. Call peers, do not reallocate.

## What budgets do show

Public over performers spent 1.26 times as much on instruction as public under performers, 1.53 on academic support, 1.29 on student services. Private over performers: 1.45, 1.46 and 0.94. After Benjamini and Hochberg correction across 100 comparisons, instruction and academic support gaps survive everywhere, student services nowhere.

**Method.** Same school completion within six years, predicted from Pell share, admission rate, enrollment, part time, first gen, adult and online shares, HBCU and Carnegie type at entry (2017 to 2018), slopes by sector (R² 0.58). Residual = actual minus predicted, shrunk for small cohorts. P values: spending <0.001, academic support share 0.003, instruction share <0.001.

## Limitations

1. **School level data.** Institutions, not students. Unmeasured program mix or preparation can produce a residual unrelated to practice. 159 special focus, adult, online and shared campus schools are scored, not ranked.
2. **Correlation only.** The 1.35 times instruction gap describes who over performs, not what to change. Moving dollars may change nothing.
3. **Who is missing.** Transfer out completers count as non completers. Privacy suppression removes 243 schools, median 222 undergraduates, 94% private, so the ranking over represents larger, public colleges.
