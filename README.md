# Which 4-year colleges graduate first-generation students above prediction, and how much of it is money?

**Live dashboard:** https://first-gen-beating-odds.streamlit.app

Some colleges graduate far more of their first-generation students than you would expect from who they enroll, and this project finds them and asks whether money is the reason. The answer: how much a college spends, and what it spends it on, explains only about 5 percent of why some schools beat the odds. The takeaway for a college that wants to do better is to learn from the peer schools that beat the odds with similar students, not to move money between budget lines.

Portfolio project. Python · DuckDB · pandas · statsmodels · plotly · Streamlit.

## Question

Which US 4-year colleges graduated first-generation students at higher rates than their student mix and structure predict, and how did their spending differ, during the years those students were enrolled, from schools that under-performed?

## Headline results (all numbers generated by the pipeline; see `reports/results.json`)

- **Effect size first:** spending level and mix together explain **10.0%** of the variance in the over-performance residual. One SD more academic-support share ≈ +1.1 points, one SD more instruction share ≈ +1.6, doubling core spending per FTE ≈ +7 points, against a top-decile cut of +10.4 points. Most over-performance sits outside budget data, so the memo's recommendation is a peer list, not a reallocation.

- **Cohort alignment:** outcome = first-gen students who entered in AY2017-18, measured in AY2023-24 (Scorecard cohort map). Predictors are the schools' 2017-18 values; spending is the FY2018-FY2023 average per FTE.
- **Sample:** 1,142 four-year public and private nonprofit colleges (Carnegie doctoral, master's, baccalaureate). Scored separately: 50 special-focus schools, 49 campuses with >50% of undergraduates aged 25+ or exclusively online, 4 schools with implausible Scorecard completion rates (e.g. San Jose State's 3.8%).
- **Model:** OLS of first-gen same-institution 6-year completion on Pell share, admission rate, log enrollment, part-time share, first-gen share, share aged 25+, share exclusively online, HBCU and Carnegie group, with private × every trait (**R² = 0.579**; pooled version 0.552), HC3 robust SEs. The interaction model is the ranking model because its top decile overlaps the sector-only rankings at 96% (public) and 97% (private). Spending is not in the model.
- **Ranking:** residual = actual − predicted, shrunk toward zero for small cohorts (empirical Bayes; τ = 9.3 pts). Shrinkage removed 10 small-cohort schools from the top decile. Top decile (115 schools) beat prediction by **≥ 10.4 points**.
- **Spending, by sector:** in a public-only model, over-performing publics spent more on all four core functions (instruction 1.26×, academic support 1.53×, student services 1.29×). In a private-only model the gap is instruction 1.45× and academic support 1.46× with no student-services difference. Pooled: instruction 1.35×, academic support 1.61× (p < 0.001), student services 1.04×.
- **Slopes differ by sector:** joint Wald F test of the eight private × trait terms, F(8, 1122) = 7.38, p < 0.001. A fractional-logit version of the model ranks schools identically in practice (Spearman 0.999, top-decile overlap 96%).
- **Level vs mix:** regressing the shrunken residual on log core spending, instruction share and academic-support share (sector and Carnegie controls): shares remain significant with the level fixed (instruction share p = 0.001, academic support p = 0.003), but level explains more (R² 0.087 vs 0.023).
- **Robustness:** a Benjamini-Hochberg correction across 100 spending tests keeps the instruction and academic-support gaps everywhere and the student-services gap nowhere; dropping 78 Cook's-flagged schools leaves them at 1.45× / 1.48×. Results hold with FY2018-19 spending only (1.40× / 1.54×), in constant FY2023 dollars (1.35× / 1.60×), and within each Carnegie group. A 1,000-resample bootstrap keeps top-decile schools in the top decile 74% of the time.

## What changed after review (see `decisions.md`)

Five review passes tested timing, sample composition, small school noise, sector differences, and multiple comparisons. Every change sits in `decisions.md`. Only 3 of the original top-20 schools survive in the corrected ranking (Spearman 0.62); the instruction and academic-support finding held through every change. The original ranking is archived in `reports/archive/`.

## Reproduce

```bash
python3 -m venv .venv && source .venv/bin/activate
pip install -r requirements.txt          # or requirements.lock.txt for the exact versions used (Python 3.14)
for s in src/0[1-8]_*.py src/10_memo.py; do python "$s"; done
streamlit run app/app.py
```

Raw data (about 876 MB) is gitignored and re-downloaded by the first script. The cleaned outputs in `data/clean/` are committed, so the dashboard and all reports work without downloading anything.

## Deploy the dashboard (Streamlit Community Cloud)

1. Push this repository to GitHub (public or private; Community Cloud can read either).
2. At share.streamlit.io choose **New app**, select the repo and branch, set **Main file path** to `app/app.py`.
3. Under **Advanced settings** pick Python 3.12 or 3.13 (Cloud does not offer 3.14 yet) and leave the requirements file as `requirements.txt`; the lock file is for local reproduction on 3.14.
4. Deploy. The app reads `data/clean/analysis_scored.parquet`, `data/clean/bootstrap_ranks.csv` and `reports/results.json` from the repo, so no secrets, databases or downloads are needed.
5. After any pipeline rerun, commit the updated `data/clean/` and `reports/results.json`; Cloud redeploys on push.

## Dashboard

Live at https://first-gen-beating-odds.streamlit.app. Locally, `streamlit run app/app.py`: actual-vs-predicted scatter with hover, coefficient chart, filterable ranking (state, sector, Carnegie group, size, Pell share) with raw and shrunken residuals, bootstrap rank intervals and CSV download, spending comparison (pooled, sector models, Carnegie groups, timing variants), per-school lookup with peer comparison, methods and limitations.

## Limitations

- **School-level data.** Institutions, not students, are the unit. Unobserved differences in program mix and preparation can produce a residual unrelated to what the school does. Special-focus colleges are excluded for this reason and listed in `reports/05_regression.md`.
- **Correlation only.** Spending differences describe over-performers; they do not show that changing spending would change completion.
- **Outcome definition.** Completion at the same institution for federal-aid students; transfer-out completers count as non-completers. Privacy suppression removes the smallest schools.
- **Structural traits** (control, level, Title IV status, open admission) come from 2023 IPEDS files and are assumed unchanged since 2017.
- **Opportunity Insights** data describe students born 1980-82 and are used as context only.
