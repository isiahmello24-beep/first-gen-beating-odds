# Which four year colleges graduate first gen students above prediction, and how much of it is money?

**Live dashboard:** https://first-gen-beating-odds.streamlit.app

Some colleges graduate far more of their first gen students than you would expect from who they enroll. This project finds them and asks how much of the gap lines up with spending. The answer: how much a college spends, and what it spends it on, explains about 10 percent of why some schools beat the odds. A college that wants to do better can learn more from the peers that beat the odds with similar students than from moving money between budget lines.

Portfolio project by Isiah Luna, a first gen transfer student at SDSU. Python, DuckDB, pandas, statsmodels, plotly, Streamlit.

## Question

Which US four year colleges graduated first gen students at higher rates than their student mix and structure predict, and how did their spending differ, during the years those students were enrolled, from schools that fell short?

## Headline results (every number is generated by the pipeline from `reports/results.json`)

- How much money explains: spending level and mix together explain **10.0%** of the variance the ranking model leaves unexplained (partial R² of nested models). Doubling core spending per FTE is associated with about +6.5 points, one SD more academic support share with +1.1 and one SD more instruction share with +1.6, against a top decile cut of +10.4 points. Most over performance sits outside budget data, so the memo's recommendation is a peer list, not a reallocation.
- Cohort alignment: the outcome is first gen students who entered in the 2017 to 2018 academic year, measured in 2023 to 2024 (Scorecard cohort map). Predictors are the schools' values in the entry year. Spending is the FY2018 to FY2023 average per FTE.
- Sample: 1,142 four year public and private nonprofit colleges (Carnegie doctoral, master's, baccalaureate). Scored but not ranked: 50 special focus or tribal schools, 49 campuses with more than 50% of undergraduates aged 25 or older or exclusively online, 4 schools with implausible Scorecard completion rates (San Jose State's 3.8% against an IPEDS graduation rate of 68%), and 56 campuses whose first gen outcome is pooled with other campuses under one OPEID (a campus is kept only if it holds at least 90% of its group's undergraduates).
- Model: OLS of first gen same institution six year completion on Pell share, admission rate, log enrollment, part time share, first gen share, share aged 25 or older, share exclusively online, HBCU and Carnegie group, with private × every trait (R² 0.579, pooled version 0.552), HC3 robust SEs. The interaction model is the ranking model because its top decile overlaps the sector only rankings at 96% (public) and 97% (private). Spending is not in the model.
- Ranking: residual = actual minus predicted, shrunk toward zero for small cohorts (empirical Bayes, τ = 9.3 points). Shrinkage removed 10 small cohort schools from the top decile. The top decile (115 schools) beat prediction by 10.4 points or more.
- Spending, by sector: among public colleges, the top decile of the main ranking spent 1.26 times as much as the bottom decile on instruction, 1.53 times on academic support and 1.29 times on student services. Among private colleges the gap is 1.45 times on instruction and 1.46 times on academic support, with student services at 0.94 times. Pooled: instruction 1.35 times, academic support 1.61 times, student services 1.04 times.
- Slopes differ by sector: joint Wald F test of the eight private × trait terms, F(8, 1122) = 7.38, p < 0.001. A fractional logit version of the model ranks schools the same in practice (Spearman 0.999, top decile overlap 96%).
- Level vs mix: in the nested model, both shares stay significant with the level fixed (instruction share p = 0.001, academic support p = 0.003), and the level explains more (partial R² 0.087 for the level alone against 0.023 for the mix alone). The earlier regression of the shrunken residual on raw spending (R² 0.060) is kept in Step 7 as a sensitivity.
- Robustness: a Benjamini and Hochberg correction across 100 spending tests keeps the instruction and academic support gaps in every comparison and the student services gap in none. Dropping the 78 schools flagged by Cook's distance (4/n rule) leaves them at 1.45 and 1.48 times. The gaps hold with FY2018 to FY2019 spending only (1.40 and 1.54 times), in constant FY2023 dollars (1.35 and 1.60 times), and within each Carnegie group.
- Ranking stability: a 1,000 resample bootstrap redraws each school's graduation count from Binomial(cohort, rate) inside every resample, so the intervals carry each school's own sampling noise as well as the uncertainty in the prediction line. Top decile schools stay in the top decile 74% of the time, and the rank interval of a typical top decile school spans 218.1 positions (median width) out of 1,142.

## What changed after review (see `decisions.md`)

Six review passes tested timing, sample composition, small school noise, sector differences, multiple comparisons and the campus grain of the outcome. Every change sits in `decisions.md`. Only 3 of the original top 20 schools survive in the corrected ranking (Spearman 0.62). The instruction and academic support finding held through every change. The earlier rankings are archived in `reports/archive/`.

## Reproduce

```bash
python3 -m venv .venv && source .venv/bin/activate
pip install -r requirements.txt          # or requirements.lock.txt for the exact versions used (Python 3.14)
for s in src/0[1-8]_*.py src/10_memo.py; do python "$s"; done
python src/check_numbers.py              # every number in the memo, README and dashboard text must trace to results.json
streamlit run app/app.py
```

Raw data (about 876 MB) is gitignored and re downloaded by the first script. The cleaned outputs in `data/clean/` are committed, so the dashboard and all reports work without downloading anything.

## Deploy the dashboard (Streamlit Community Cloud)

1. Push this repository to GitHub (public or private, Community Cloud can read either).
2. At share.streamlit.io choose New app, select the repo and branch, and set the main file path to `app/app.py`.
3. Under advanced settings pick Python 3.12 or 3.13 (Cloud does not offer 3.14 yet) and leave the requirements file as `requirements.txt`. The lock file is for local reproduction on 3.14.
4. Deploy. The app reads `data/clean/analysis_scored.parquet`, `data/clean/bootstrap_ranks.csv` and `reports/results.json` from the repo, so no secrets, databases or downloads are needed.
5. After any pipeline rerun, commit the updated `data/clean/` and `reports/results.json`. Cloud redeploys on push.

## Limitations

- **School level data.** Institutions, not students, are the unit. Differences in program mix and preparation that the predictors do not capture can produce a residual unrelated to what the school does. Special focus colleges are set aside for this reason and listed in `reports/05_regression.md`.
- **Correlation only.** Spending differences describe over performers. They do not show that changing spending would change completion.
- **Outcome definition.** Completion at the same institution for federal aid students. Transfer out completers count as non completers.
- **Who is missing.** The 243 privacy suppressed schools have a median of 222 undergraduates and are 94% private, against 2,790.5 and 60% in the main sample, so the ranking over represents larger and public colleges and says nothing about colleges with fewer than 50 first gen federal aid students in the cohort.
- **Structural traits** (control, level, Title IV status, open admission) come from 2023 IPEDS files and are assumed unchanged since 2017.
- **Opportunity Insights** data describe students born 1980 to 1982 and are used as context only.
