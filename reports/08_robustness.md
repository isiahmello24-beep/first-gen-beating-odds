## (a) Bootstrap of residual ranks: 1,000 resamples of 1,142 schools (seed 20261023)

Each resample redraws every school's rate from Binomial(cohort, observed rate), draws the schools that define the prediction line with replacement, refits the ranking model, re-applies the empirical-Bayes shrinkage and re-ranks every school. `p_top_decile` = share of resamples in which the school lands in the top 10%.

- Point-estimate top decile: 114 schools. Their mean probability of being top-decile across resamples: **74.0%**.
- 32% of them are top-decile in ≥ 90% of resamples; 86% in ≥ 50%.
- 0 schools outside the point-estimate top decile are top-decile in ≥ 50% of resamples (borderline cases).
- Mean overlap of the top decile between two random resamples: 64.3%.
- Median width of the 95% rank interval: 218 rank positions for top-decile schools, 405 for all schools (out of 1,142).

Reading this: the coefficients are estimated from 1,142 schools, so resampling the schools moves the prediction line only a little; most of the interval width comes from the binomial redraw of each school's own rate, which is largest for small cohorts. The Step 6 shrinkage pulls small-cohort residuals toward zero for the same reason; the bootstrap shows how much rank uncertainty remains after it.

## (d) Fractional logit version of the main model

Same predictors, binomial family with a logit link (quasi-likelihood for a 0-1 proportion, robust SEs). Deviance pseudo-R² = 0.566. Shrunken residuals from the logit fit vs the main OLS ranking: Spearman 0.998, top-decile overlap 96%. A logit keeps predictions inside 0-1 and lets effects shrink near the bounds; with an outcome that ranges 7-95% and no pile-up at either end, it should agree with OLS, and it does.
