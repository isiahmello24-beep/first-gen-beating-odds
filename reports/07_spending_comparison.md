## 7b (sensitivity). Shrunken residual regressed on raw spending (n = 1,142, HC3)

The headline "money explains X%" is Step 5's Model B-mix (partial R² against the ranking model). This older version regresses the residual on raw spending and understates the coefficient, because spending overlaps the traits already in the model; it is kept to show the difference.

`residual_eb ~ log_core_per_fte + instr_share + acad_share + private + C(carnegie_group, Treatment(reference="Master's"))`


| term                           |    coef |   robust_se |      p |
|:-------------------------------|--------:|------------:|-------:|
| intercept                      | -0.5244 |      0.065  | 0      |
| log core $/FTE                 |  0.0494 |      0.0068 | 0      |
| instruction share of core      |  0.0541 |      0.0343 | 0.1145 |
| academic-support share of core |  0.1311 |      0.0515 | 0.0109 |
| private                        | -0.0044 |      0.0077 | 0.5642 |


R² = 0.060 (level only 0.054; mix only 0.017). Holding total core spending per student fixed, one standard deviation more instruction share (0.102) is associated with +0.55 points of residual (p = 0.114); one SD more academic-support share (0.058) with +0.76 points (p = 0.011). A 10% higher core budget is associated with +0.47 points (p = 0.000). Verdict in this sensitivity: the mix matters with the level fixed. The memo reads Step 5.

Interpretation guide: `welch_p_log` tests whether mean log-dollars differ (robust to the long right tail); `mann_whitney_p` tests whether one group's values tend to be larger. Dollar differences are the finding. Shares describe how the same dollars are split and cannot be read as separate evidence. Mann-Whitney is the decision test; Welch is shown beside it.
